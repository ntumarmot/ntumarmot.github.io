## My history with computers

I started using computers in elementary school. My first one was an ASUS laptop. I no longer remember its screen size; I only remember that the screen felt huge and the laptop was heavy for an elementary school student. My family bought it so I could learn programming, and it stayed with me through middle and high school whenever I needed to work on a report.

In my first year of university, I bought a new silver Acer Swift with 16 GB of memory and 512 GB of storage. I used it for only a year before selling it for several reasons:

1. It became very slow when I had several programs open, and the fan would spin nonstop and get very loud
2. The battery drained quickly and lasted only about two hours
3. My eyes would start hurting for no clear reason after looking at the screen for one or two hours, and I would need to rest for quite a while before they felt better
4. More and more people around me were switching to Macs, and I wanted to try one too

In my second year, I bought a 13-inch M2 MacBook Air, also with 16 GB of memory and 512 GB of storage. I was actually very happy with that computer and used it until the summer after my third year. After switching to a Mac, I stopped having the eye pain I had experienced before, and I was also very satisfied with the battery life. In daily use, fan noise was no longer an issue, and the computer rarely became hot. It felt very different from using the Acer.

Later, during my internship, the company did not provide a computer or a large external monitor. I had to look at my own laptop for around ten hours every day, so I began to think that a larger screen could improve my productivity and reduce the strain on my eyes.

This July, I bought a used 2021 16-inch MacBook Pro with the M1 Pro chip for less than NT$40,000. Considering its specifications and display, I think it was a pretty good deal. It is also the second MacBook I have owned.

## The large screen really does feel great

My most immediate reaction after switching to 16 inches was simple: the large screen feels great.

The difference from a 13-inch display is especially noticeable when I watch videos or use two apps side by side. I still remember watching the first episode of *Mushoku Tensei* Season 3 during my first few days with the laptop. The experience on this display was something a 13-inch laptop simply could not match.

The 120 Hz display also feels incredibly smooth. I notice the difference when scrolling through webpages or switching between windows. Once I got used to 120 Hz, it became hard to go back.

## Programming and studying Japanese are both easier

I now do most of my programming with Codex or Claude Code, which means I work directly in the terminal. On the 16-inch display, I can make the terminal larger and see more content at once instead of squeezing all the text into a small window.

The larger screen is also very useful when I study Japanese. I can study grammar or read an article on the left half of the screen while keeping GPT or Gemini open on the right to explain anything I do not understand. I could also do this on a 13-inch display, but on the 16-inch screen, both sides remain at a much more comfortable size.

## Other things I have noticed

The battery lasts at least five or six hours, which is already enough for me. The keyboard also feels good to type on. Both MacBooks I have used have had keyboards that suited me better than the one on my Acer.

As for how much faster the M1 Pro is than my previous M2 MacBook Air, I still do not have a clear answer, so I do not want to make a definite claim. Everything I do in daily use has been smooth, and the computer has hardly ever felt hot. However, the next time I buy a computer, I would like to choose one with 32 GB of memory because I want to try running models locally.

## The biggest downside is the weight

There is, of course, a downside: this laptop is heavy. It weighs more than two kilograms, nearly one kilogram more than my previous MacBook Air. I can definitely feel the difference once it is in my backpack, and the backpack becomes genuinely heavy.

I even switched back to the MacBook Air for a week, and it felt incredibly light. However, I eventually returned to the MacBook Pro. The weight is an obvious disadvantage, but for now, the 16-inch MacBook is still the one I use more often.

## If I had to choose again

The next time I replace my computer, I will probably want a 15-inch MacBook Air. Its screen is larger than the 13-inch model, but it is not as heavy as the 16-inch MacBook Pro. It seems like a better balance between screen size and weight.

I think the 16-inch MacBook Pro is a good fit for people who need to use two apps side by side, really want a large screen, and do not mind carrying some extra weight every day. If you often take your laptop outside and dislike having a heavy backpack, you should think carefully before choosing it.
