# Why I started learning Japanese and what I learned

I have been interested in Japanese for a long time, mainly because I watched a lot of Japanese anime in elementary and middle school. I did not have much time to study Japanese in middle school, but in high school I chose it as my second foreign language.

To be honest, the full year of Japanese I took in tenth grade did not leave much in my memory. I remember getting a perfect score on the kana test, but by the second semester I still could not read kana without checking a chart and writing down the pronunciation.

Time passed quickly. I took required English courses in my first year of university and had several demanding department requirements in my second year. In my third year, I finally enrolled in Japanese again.

As I mentioned in another blog post, I thought my Japanese I instructor taught very well. If you have not seen that post, it probably means I have not written it yet. XDD As a result, I built a fairly solid foundation.

By the end of Japanese I, we had covered what I estimate to be around 80 percent of N5 material. That is only my own estimate, so it may not be exact. The grammar points I particularly remember not covering were:

- てもいいです
- てはいけません
- てはかまいません

There were also vocabulary gaps, such as 川（かわ）.

For material that slips through the cracks, I recommend searching online for an N5 grammar list before the exam and reviewing it quickly. Vocabulary requires steady accumulation, so tools such as Anki can help. I plan to write a separate introduction to it, although there are already many useful guides online.

I did not buy any practice books before the exam. I relied entirely on conversations with AI and free questions I found online. I think that is enough for N5 because questions at this level are generally straightforward. If you have seen the grammar point before, you can usually answer the question.

## The main event: listening

My classes did not place special emphasis on listening practice. Listening appeared in exams, and the instructor shared all audio files covered by each test beforehand.

I searched YouTube for N5 listening practice. I first listened once and checked whether I understood the meaning. Whether I understood it or not, I always read the transcript afterward. The transcript is essential; I do not think a listening exercise without one is very useful. I looked up unfamiliar vocabulary and grammar, then listened again without reading. I made sure I could understand everything before moving to the next question.
